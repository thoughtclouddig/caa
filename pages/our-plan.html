Our Plan
Where Faith Takes Flight

The plan to become the worldwide home for every Catholic in aviation.

CAA exists to help Catholics find fellowship, Mass, mentorship, service, and spiritual encouragement wherever aviation takes them. This is our plan to build the home that mission has always needed.

The Gap We're Closing

Aviation is one of the loneliest professions to practice a faith in.

Whatever you do in aviation, the schedule rarely cooperates with Sunday. A pilot, a mechanic, a controller, a flight attendant, a missionary aviator: the shift changes, but the isolation doesn't.

"Right now, there is no global, credible, easy-to-find place where a Catholic in any of these roles can say: here is where I belong."

The Church is universal. This community should be too. We already have chapters, a chapel directory, member benefits, prayer resources, and events. What we don't have yet is a way for a first officer landing in Nairobi to find Mass in under a minute, or a mechanic to find an experienced mentor, or a missionary pilot to receive prayer support overnight. That's what this plan builds.

What Guides Us

Four things we don't compromise on.

Faith

Rooted, not vague. Catholic first: Mass, the sacraments, and the Church's own aviation patrons treated with the same seriousness as any parish.

Fellowship

The antidote to isolation. Every feature we build answers one question: does this help two Catholics in aviation find each other?

Vocation

A calling, not just a job. We treat aviation as work worth sanctifying, which is what separates a community from an affinity group.

Service

The default posture. From mission aviation to a mentor's midnight reply, service isn't a program here. It's the culture.

What We're Building

A platform for daily engagement, not annual renewal.

A new member's first year should move through discovery, free membership, finding their people, and going deeper, not a one-time sign-up that gets forgotten. Six things make that possible:

Everyday

Airport Chapel & Mass Finder

The single most practical problem in a traveling Catholic's life, solved. Used on every trip, not once.

Belonging

Worldwide Member Map

Turns an abstract "global community" into something you can actually see and zoom into.

Formation

Matches students and newcomers with veterans by role and specialty. An ongoing relationship, not a form.

Local

Chapter Directory

Turns a global idea into a Tuesday-night meeting near you. The on-ramp to physical community.

Real people, real vocations. The moment a visitor thinks, "that could be me."

The most intimate form of belonging the site can offer. Daily engagement rooted in genuine care, not gimmicks.

How This Grows

We grow through people personally inviting people, not advertising.

Community actually happens at a chapter meeting, in a mentorship, in a chaplain's office, not in a mass sign-up. So instead of buying ads, we're growing through the people who already know these Catholics personally: chaplains, flight instructors, chapter leaders, and members inviting the next one.

Chaplaincy & Diocesan Lists

Direct outreach through the military and airport chaplains, and diocesan vocations offices, who already know these Catholics by name.

Flight Schools & Aviation Universities

Campus reps and orientation-week outreach, to catch the vocation before it scatters.

Professional Targeting

Role- and employer-based outreach to pilots, mechanics, dispatchers, and flight attendants at named carriers.

Trade & Airline-Crew Presence

Tables at aviation trade shows and airline Catholic-group meetups.

Partner & Affinity Lists

Co-marketing with aviation insurers, headset makers, and training academies.

Ambassador-Seeded Referral

Airport ambassadors and chapter leads personally inviting the next member. The highest-converting channel we have, and free.

The Church has never needed a headquarters to be universal. It only ever needed its people to find each other.

We don't need to invent a mission. We already have one, and it's a good one. What we need is the courage to build a home large enough for it to live in: an association that maintains a membership list, or a movement that Catholics in aviation, anywhere in the world, call home. We believe the second is already half built. This is the plan to finish it.
