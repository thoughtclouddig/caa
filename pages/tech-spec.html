CAA Brand & Website Overview

The brand system (logo, colors, typography) and how the new website works: what's being built, how content gets managed day to day, and what it replaces. No jargon, no assumptions about technical background.

What this document covers.

The mockups you've seen so far were about look and feel: layout, tone, navigation. This document is about the machinery underneath, where content lives, who can edit what, and how the pieces fit together. Nothing here changes CAA's mission, membership structure, or content. It's the technical plan for building what we've already discussed.

The logo direction still needs a decision.

This is not an approved logo. It takes the same figure of Christ from CAA's current logo and rebuilds it as clean vector artwork, so it stays sharp at any size and can be used in print, embroidery, and digital without redrawing it every time. CAA needs to decide on a direction before any of this is locked in.

One deliberate change is worth calling out. In the current logo, Christ sits below the engine of the aircraft. In this direction, Christ is moved to the top of the mark with the halo as the highest element, so nothing in the artwork sits above Him. That is a meaningful change, not a cosmetic one, and it is the main thing to react to.

Understanding the marketing funnel.

A "marketing funnel" just describes the stages someone goes through on the way to joining anything: wide at the top, meaning people who've never heard of you, narrow at the bottom, meaning people who've actually taken action. Every page on the site has a different job depending on where it sits in that funnel.

Homepage

Awareness

Someone has never heard of CAA. The only job here is: explain what this is, in a few seconds, and get them to become a free member.

Member Portal

Engagement

Someone already joined. Now the job is to give them a reason to come back: chapter, mentorship, prayer, formation.

Give / Membership

Commitment

Someone already engaged. Now the job is inviting deeper commitment: paid membership, giving, Patron.

This matters because it changes what "good" looks like on the homepage specifically. It isn't there to explain every program CAA runs or show everything at once. It's there to convert a cold visitor into a free member as fast and clearly as possible. Anything that slows that down or dilutes the message works against the page's actual job, even if it looks nice.

Why No Image Slider In The Header

Rotating hero sliders are a common request, but the research on them is consistent: most visitors never see past the first slide. They either act on the first message or leave within a few seconds, well before a slider rotates. A slider that cycles through 3-4 messages effectively means 2-3 of them are seen by almost nobody, and it splits attention away from the one thing the homepage actually needs someone to do. A single, static, strong message with one clear call to action converts better at the top of the funnel than a rotating carousel does. That is why the homepage mock uses one headline and one CTA, not a slideshow.

Every real membership site has these three parts.

Right now, catholicaviation.org blends all three into one platform (eCatholic). The new system separates them clearly, which is what makes it flexible and fast to build.

Layer 1

Public Website

What anyone finds through Google before they've ever heard of CAA: Home, Participate, Resources, About, Chapters. Its entire job is to explain what CAA is and get someone to register or join.

Layer 2

Member Portal

What a member sees after logging in: the directory, chapter tools, mentorship, Witness formation, prayer requests, event RSVPs, and their own membership/dues status.

Layer 3

Content & Admin Tools

The behind-the-scenes tools staff use to update the public site, publish articles, manage chapters and events, and review new member registrations, without needing a developer.

Staff can edit content. They can't change the layout.

We're using a content tool called Sanity to manage everything editable. The important thing isn't the name. It's what it does: it lets staff change specific pieces of text, swap photos, and write full articles with a normal rich-text editor (bold, links, headings, images, like writing an email), without being able to accidentally break the page layout or the brand design.

Staff get control over content. The design stays exactly as it was built, every time.

This is a deliberate choice. A tool that lets anyone freely rearrange sections and layouts (like some popular website builders) also lets anyone accidentally make the site look inconsistent or off-brand over time. Locking the layout down and opening up only the content keeps CAA's site looking professional permanently, not just on launch day.
